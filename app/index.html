What is Perturb-seq?

Perturb-seq pairs a pooled CRISPR screen with single-cell RNA-seq. Each cell receives a guide RNA that switches off one gene, and its whole transcriptome is read out together with the identity of that guide. One experiment therefore measures what happens to every other gene when each of thousands of genes is knocked down.

This site turns the published results into something you can browse: pick a gene, see which genes respond most, and which pathways those responding genes share.

Reading the results

  • Knockdown is not knockout. The median knockdown is 83%, but 238 perturbations reduced their target by less than half. Check the knockdown figure before reading anything downstream.
  • Many knockdowns change little. 78 perturbations have no significantly changed gene. That is often the true answer, not a fault of the page.
  • Very few cells, less certainty. 64 perturbations were measured in fewer than 25 cells, fewer than any control used to check the test, so their significance calls are less certain (see Checks).
  • Downstream is not direct. A changed gene may sit several regulatory steps away from the one knocked down.
  • One cancer cell line, one time point. Effects may differ in other cell types, or at other times after knockdown.
